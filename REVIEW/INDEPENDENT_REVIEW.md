## 3. Assessment of the Methodological Reconstruction

The paper correctly distinguishes between:

- **Conceptual continuity:** the preservation of the original questions.
- **Epistemological continuity:** the inheritance of premises, methods, or conclusions.

The bracketing of metaphysical premises (Section 5.1) is a **valid methodological operation**, not a rejection. The paper is explicit that the original comparative formulation is a source of questions, not a source of answers. This distinction is critical, and the paper maintains it consistently.

**Verdict:** The methodological reconstruction is sound. The paper does not commit the fallacy of begging the question (Section 5.2).

---

## 4. Assessment of the Four-Level Stratification

The four analytical levels (Psychological Decision-Making, Decision Authority, Normative Authority, Sovereignty Architecture) are presented as **analytical coordinates**, not as evidence of four independent components.

The paper explicitly states:

> "The existence of these four distinctions does not constitute evidence of an independent architectural entity. They serve strictly as analytical coordinates to test a core structural dilemma." (Section 6.2)

This guardrail is **appropriately stated and consistently maintained**. The paper does not commit the fallacy of reification.

**Verdict:** The four-level stratification is a legitimate analytical tool. It does not, by itself, establish the independence of sovereignty.

---

## 5. Assessment of the Testing Dichotomy

The paper specifies two competing hypotheses:

- **H1 — Emergent Reducibility:** sovereignty is an emergent descriptive label.
- **H2 — Irreducible Architectural Responsibility:** sovereignty adds a structural responsibility that cannot be fully reconstructed.

The paper acknowledges the **asymmetry** between the two hypotheses:

> "H1 can be supported by demonstrating that the four-level description collapses into a three-level description without loss. H2 cannot be supported by the mere absence of such a demonstration; it requires a positive specification of what level 4 adds." (Section 7.2)

This asymmetry is **methodologically correct**. The paper does not treat the current inability to reconstruct level 4 as evidence of irreducibility. This is a common error in architectural proposals, and the paper avoids it explicitly.

**Verdict:** The testing dichotomy is well-specified. The asymmetry is correctly identified and respected.

---

## 6. Assessment of the Falsification Criteria

The paper specifies three falsification criteria:

- **AS-F1 (Functional Duplication):** the five functions of sovereignty are already covered by existing dimensions.
- **AS-F2 (Reconstructability):** the four-level stratification is fully reconstructible from levels 1–3.
- **AS-F5 (Emergent Epistemic Reducibility):** sovereignty adds no irreducible structural responsibility.

None of these criteria has been tested. The paper states this explicitly:

> "None has yet been tested." (Abstract)

> "All three conditions are untested." (Section 8.1)

This is **methodologically honest**. The paper does not claim that "not falsified" means "confirmed." The distinction is maintained throughout.

**Verdict:** The falsification criteria are well-specified. The current status ("not falsified, untested") is accurately reported.

---

## 7. Assessment of the Evaluation Design

The evaluation design (S1–S6, M1–M6) is presented as a **proposal, not an executed study**. The paper states:

> "The evaluation design is a proposal, not an executed study. No scenario has been run. No metric has been measured." (Section 11.5)

This is **methodologically correct**. The design is specified so that future work has a concrete target, not so that the current investigation can claim empirical support.

**Verdict:** The evaluation design is a legitimate proposal. It does not constitute evidence for or against the candidate.

---

## 8. Assessment of the Threats to Validity

The paper identifies seven threats to validity (T1–T7), including:

- **T1:** Author-on-author reasoning (High).
- **T2:** No independent review (High) — *this review addresses T2 partially, but does not eliminate it.*
- **T6:** No measurement (High).
- **T7:** Unresolved Govern and Identity (High).

The threats are **documented openly and accurately**. The paper does not minimize them.

**Verdict:** The threats-to-validity register is complete and honest. The paper does not overstate the strength of its conclusions.

## 10. Additional Recommendations (Non-Binding)

The following recommendations are offered as suggestions, not as conditions of approval:

1. **Clarify the boundary with AetherGovern:** The paper acknowledges that the boundary is unresolved (T7). A future version could include a more detailed comparison of sovereignty and governance.

2. **Strengthen AS-F1:** The functional duplication criterion could be operationalized more concretely (e.g., a function-by-function matrix).

3. **Address T1 (author-on-author reasoning):** This review partially addresses T2, but T1 remains. A second independent review (by a different reviewer) would strengthen the investigation.

4. **Consider a formal proof sketch for P2:** The proposition that "there exists an irreducible human authority anchor" is currently stated but not proven. A formal proof sketch would clarify its status.
